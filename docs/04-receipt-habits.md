# Chapter 4: Shoebox-to-System Receipt Habits

> **What this chapter is:** a kind, low-effort path that takes receipts from "at the bottom of a coat pocket" to "in an organized place a CPA can actually use." **What it is not:** a lecture about how you *should* already be doing this. Nobody already is. That's the whole point of the chapter.

## The shoebox, part one: why it's actually a decent start

Here's an uncomfortable confession from the entire accounting profession: a shoebox **works**. Why? Because it does the two things a receipt system needs at minimum:

1. **Nothing gets thrown away.**
2. **It's all in one place.**

Those two properties put you ahead of the drawer-of-doom, the wallet-graveyard, and the pants-that-never-got-washed. The system in this chapter is just a shoebox with two upgrades: **dates** and **minutes**. (Yes, literal minutes — the whole system runs on ~2 of them per day.)

## The daily two minutes: "drop and dot"

The habit that carries everything:

1. **DROP:** the moment money leaves your hands (or a receipt hits your inbox), the receipt goes to *one* place. Choose the place today: a kitchen counter tray, a phone-photos album, or a single email folder. It does not matter which — **it matters that it's always the same one.**
2. **DOT:** before it goes in the tray/album/folder, write **two words** on it (or in the photo, or as the email's subject): *why*. "Client post," "desk part," "book shipping." Two words, that's it. This is the whole "system" part of shoebox-to-system.

Rules for the two-word dot — they exist because these are the three cases your future self will actually hit:

- **Business spend:** the purpose. "Book supplies," "AI sub," "market postage."
- **Looks-business-but-is-personal:** write "personal" anyway. An honest "personal" receipt that never enters business books is worth ten confused receipts that sort of might. Your main wallet-receipts shouldn't be in this tray at all (see "the personal/business house rule," below).
- **Not sure:** write "???" plus two words describing the thing. A marked mystery beats an unmarked one — it gets *resolved at the monthly sweep*, when memory of recent purchases is at its best.

## Digital receipts let you skip a trap beginners fall into

Most first-year receipts aren't paper anyway — they're **emails and portal downloads**: software subscriptions, platform charges, marketplace statements, hosting. Paper rules apply unchanged, with two helpings of kindness:

1. **Set up one email folder, auto-label, or rule** (name it something obvious like `biz-receipts`). Every business purchase — even ones on a personal card — sends its receipt there. Two minutes of one-time setup, then it maintains itself forever.
2. **Monthly downloads beat "I'll grab it later."** Platform statements (marketplace/royalty dashboards) and subscription summaries get downloaded once per month during the Phase 5 sweep of the monthly close (Chapter 3), straight into the folder. Leaving them "for later" is exactly where they die.

**The house rule for personal/business mixing:** life is not sterile — sometimes one card holds both worlds. When that unavoidable thing happens, keep it honest: the receipt goes in your tray **with a dot saying "personal,"** and at the sweep it gets *sorted out*, not smuggled into the business folder. The system's entire value is its honesty; a folder with a couple of personal items wrong-homed is worth slightly less than no folder at all.

## The monthly sweep — where the shoebox upgrades

This is Phase 5 of your monthly close (Chapter 3), shown close-up because it's where the system pays rent:

```
THE MONTHLY SWEEP, in five moves
1. Gather: everything in the tray + new email-folder arrivals + ecosystem downloads
2. Date: file each into THIS month's folder / envelope / photo-album page
3. Dot-check: any receipt still missing its two words gets them now
4. Throw away: exactly one category — receipts already satisfactorily
   digitized/re-filed (duplicates, "personal" ones you already excluded)
5. Near-empty goal: the tray should END the sweep almost empty
```

The tray's emptiness at month's end is the habit's smoke detector. A tray still full on sweep day isn't a failure — it's information: the *drop* habit is slipping, so make the tray more convenient (different room, different hour).

## The year-end pay-off: the shoebox, transformed

Here's what the CPA-day version of your system looks like after 12 sweeps:

```
YEAR FOLDER
├── 01-january/   (paper scans, email PDFs, downloaded statements)
├── 02-february/
├── 03-march/
│   ...
└── 12-december/
```

Everything inside is either dotted with two words or consciously marked "???-resolved," each month's items tie to that month's ledger lines, and every platform statement you downloaded is inside. For a CPA, this is the difference between **"let's talk an hour to sort this"** and **"this is clean, my work here is quick."** Chapter 5 hands them this folder and lets them drive.

## If you fall off the wagon (you will; plan for it)

Systems for humans need re-entry ramps, not just entrances:

- **Missed a few days:** do a 10-minute "sweep anyway" — dump pocket/email dribs into the tray, dot what you remember, mark the rest "???". The tray heals.
- **Missed a whole month:** double-sweep at the next close (Chapter 3 covers how two closes coexist). Expect more "???" marks; that's fine, honesty over perfection.
- **The system got boring and you stopped:** shrink it. One folder is enough if detailing feels heavy. A minimal system that runs beats a deluxe one that doesn't.

> **The fine print, warmly said:** receipts, timing, and what's *countable* can differ based on your tax situation, and none of that is decided here. This workbook builds the *organizing muscle*; whether a specific receipt "counts" is a question for a licensed professional — and thanks to this chapter, they'll have what they need, labeled, to answer it fast.
