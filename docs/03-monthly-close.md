# Chapter 3: The 30-Minute Monthly Close Ritual

> **What this chapter is:** a gentle, repeatable ritual you can do once a month, in roughly half an hour, that keeps your books honest without bulldozing your weekend. **What it is not:** an all-night accounting marathon, or tax advice.

## Why a ritual, and why monthly?

Two reasons, both deeply human:

1. **Short memory is your enemy.** Two weeks from now, "what was that $28 charge?" is solvable. Three months from now, you won't even remember which card it came from. Monthly means every mystery is still fresh.
2. **Rituals beat motivation.** On day 1 of your business, you were full of energy and spreadsheets felt exciting. By month nine, willpower is gone — only the ritual remains. A ritual needs no energy; it just needs a calendar slot.

The monthly close is also the thing that makes **tax season boring** — in the good way. When your CPA asks for a year of records (Chapter 5), you hand over twelve already-balanced months instead of one terrifying shoebox.

## The ritual, phase by phase

Print-or-copy the one-page version in `docs/07-monthly-close-checklist.md`. Here's the walkthrough, with the *why* for each step.

### Phase 1 — Gather (about 5 minutes)

**Get three things:** your bank statement (or app export) for the month, your ledger/worksheet, and your receipt stash (Chapter 4 handles how receipts *got* there — this phase just needs them present).

**Set a timer for 30 minutes.** This sounds gimmicky and works anyway. A close that runs past 30 minutes usually means something's genuinely confusing — which is a signal to *stop and sort it out with tea*, not to power through and create errors.

### Phase 2 — Match (about 10 minutes)

Go line by line down the bank statement. Each line gets one of three fates:

- **It's already in my ledger** → check the amount, ✓ it off, next line.
- **It's NOT in my ledger** → add it. Ask: which of the six accounts? Income (money in), Operating Expenses/Big Purchases (money out), Pay Me (went to personal), or a Detail note (transfers, refunds, owner contribution).
- **It's in my ledger but NOT on the statement** → usually a timing thing (you paid on the 30th, it cleared the 1st). Leave a small note: "cleared next month." No panic, no editing history.

The superpower word for this phase is **"no surprises."** When the statement and the ledger say the same story in different fonts, you're done. This act of agreeing is called **reconciliation**, and doing it monthly is what your bank *and* future-you both secretly hope for.

### Phase 3 — Foot the columns (about 5 minutes)

Add up each of the four columns that produce your P&L:

```
Income            = $___
Operating Expenses = $___
Big Purchases      = $___
Net profit (cash) = Income - Operating Expenses - Big Purchases = $___
```

Note what's *not* in that math: **Pay Me** and **Set-Aside**. Money you paid yourself isn't a business expense; the jar is still your money. Leaving them out is right, not lazy.

Write the four numbers somewhere you'll find them again — the Yearly Tracker strip on the fill-in worksheet works.

### Phase 4 — The jar check (about 3 minutes)

Look at your Set-Aside jar. Ask the only question that matters here: **"Did the jar get its slice this month?"**

- Yes → move on with a small feeling of victory.
- No → move the slice now, or write the amount owed to the jar at the top of next month's page. (The "jar IOU" is allowed. The "jar silently skipped forever" is where disasters grow.)

### Phase 5 — The receipt sweep (about 5 minutes)

Chapter 4 built you a receipt habit; this step is the monthly *emptying* of it:

1. Collect any loose receipts from wallet, car, coat pocket, email folder.
2. Photograph or sort them into the month's folder/envelope.
3. On any receipt you can't immediately connect to a business reason, write **two words** on it (or in a note): what it was *for*. Your future self in 11 months is begging you.
4. Anything genuinely personal and identical-looking to a business buy **stays out** of the business folder — lumping personal spends in creates work and confusion for your CPA, not savings.

### Phase 6 — Close the book (about 2 minutes)

Do the closing ceremony, and yes, it's allowed to be a little ceremonial:

1. Write the month's four numbers on your Yearly Tracker.
2. Date and initial the ledger page. ("Closed by me, on this day, with these numbers.") It sounds fussy; it's actually the difference between a document and a napkin.
3. One sentence in the back of the ledger: how did this month *feel* money-wise? ("Thin but OK," "royalties surprise — good.") Books with feelings get read more than books that are just numbers.

Then **stop.** Do not balance anything else, do not build a forecast, do not reorganize the jar system at 9pm. The ritual is 30 minutes *because it stops*.

## When the month goes sideways

Real months contain surprises. Three common ones and their plain-English responses:

- **"The bank statement and ledger disagree by $4."** Hunt first for: a card-verification hold, two-court-stripe fees, a forgotten subscription, an ATM fee. Small unexplained residue after an honest hunt gets a Detail note ("small unexplained — checked, left this month") — and this is exactly the kind of honest note that CPA Day (Chapter 5) rewards.
- **"I missed a whole month."** Do the missed month next, before a fresh one. Old months get stale FAST — a stale month is where receipts disappear — and closing in *order* protects the ritual feeling. Two closes in one sitting is fine and takes ~50 minutes. Just use two timer sessions with a break between.
- **"I found a receipt-tombstone: a buy I completely forgot."** Record it in its correct month. Yes, it may unbalance that month against what you already recorded — that's fine; write "late receipt, added [date]" in the Detail column. Late-and-honest always beats never.

## The 30-minute close on a calculator-vs-software question

This same ritual runs identically in three homes:

1. **On paper** — the fill-in worksheet + checklist. (Suggested for month 1 regardless. Paper teaches the moves.)
2. **In a spreadsheet** — the six accounts as columns with a totals row. Total time identical.
3. **In bookkeeping software** — matching still happens ("categorize") and math ("reports") is automated. Phases 4–6 remain yours — software does not refill the jar or run the sweep.

> **Gentle reminder:** all of the above is education, not tax advice. Where the ritual touches tax-adjacent topics (the Set-Aside jar, timing, big purchases), a licensed professional is the one permitted to make calls in your specific case. The workbook just gets you to that conversation organized and calm.
