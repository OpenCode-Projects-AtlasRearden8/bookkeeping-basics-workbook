# Glossary — Plain-English Edition

> Every term below is defined the way a friend would explain it across a kitchen table. Definitions are **education only** — none of this is tax, legal, or accounting advice, and where a word's *tax meaning* matters for your situation, a licensed professional is the voice to trust.

## The six accounts (Chapter 1)

- **Business Checking** — a real bank account that holds only business money. The spine of the whole system; personal and business money never share it.
- **Income** — the bucket for every dollar the business *earns*, counted (on cash basis) when it lands.
- **Operating Expenses** — everyday running costs you use up within the year: software, supplies, postage, subscriptions.
- **Big Purchases** — long-lived, real-cost items (laptop, desk, professional printer) separated from monthly expenses so one big buy doesn't disguise a normal month.
- **Pay Me / Owner Draws** — money you move from business to personal life. It's not an expense; it's you getting paid out of profit.
- **Set-Aside** — a labeled slice of your business checking reserved for tax season and slow months. Not a formal accounting category; a habit jar.

## Cash-basis core (Chapter 2)

- **Cash basis** — money counts when it actually moves. Income = the day it lands; expenses = the day money leaves. The solopreneur-friendly default this workbook teaches.
- **Accrual (by contrast)** — money counts when it's *earned or owed* rather than when it moves. Used by many larger companies; the thing cash basis is the simpler cousin of.
- **Lands / clears** — the day money actually arrives in (or leaves) your account, as the bank's own timestamp shows. Cash basis runs on clear dates.
- **Owner contribution** — your own money moved *into* the business. Never Income; a Detail-note event, flagged for your professional.
- **Timing wobble** — the way cash basis makes one month look thin and the next look fat, when a piece of work and its payment land in different months. Handled by reading month-pairs and quarters, never by editing history.

## The close (Chapter 3)

- **Monthly close** — a 30-minute ritual: gather, match to the bank statement, foot the columns, jar check, receipt sweep, close the book.
- **Reconciliation** — the act of making the ledger and the bank statement tell the same story. The "no surprises" step.
- **Footing the columns** — adding up each account column to produce the month's four numbers. The whole of Phase 3.
- **Jar IOU** — an honest note that the Set-Aside slice was skipped this month, written on next month's page. Allowed; permanent silence isn't.
- **Yearly Tracker** — the strip where each closed month writes its four numbers, so trends stay visible on one line.
- **Detail note** — a short line on a ledger entry explaining anything unusual ("cleared next month," "late receipt," "refund"), keeping books honest without rewriting them.

## Receipts (Chapter 4)

- **Shoebox** (term of endearment) — any one place where all business receipts go before sorting. The start of the system, and honestly not a bad start.
- **Drop** — the reflex where every receipt goes to the one place, immediately, always the same place.
- **Dot** — the two words written on (or into) each receipt saying *why*: "book shipping," "AI sub," "???" for genuinely unsure. Dots make receipts useful later.
- **Sweep** — the monthly receipt-emptying: gather, date, dot-check, discard duplicates, aim for a near-empty tray.
- **Year folder** — twelve month-folders of receipts, statements, and downloads that turns CPA Day from archaeology into sign-off.

## CPA Day (Chapter 5)

- **CPA** — Certified Public Accountant; a licensed professional whose advice is the *advice* in this workbook's "not advice" formula. Other licensed tax pros fill the same role.
- **Packing list** — the seven things you bring to a first professional visit: year P&L, bank statements, year folder, ledger, questions list, basics sheet, flagged weirdos.
- **Basics sheet** — a one-page identity card of the business (name, structure, state, key dates, bank) built once and updated rarely.
- **Questions list** — 3–6 written questions, made before the visit, that you can read aloud in two minutes.
- **Weirdos** — the odd, hard-to-categorize money events from your Detail column. Far from being embarrassments, they're what the professional is *for*.

## Reading the numbers (Chapter 6)

- **P&L (Profit and Loss)** — the one page that says what landed, what left, and whether the month ended ahead.
- **Net profit (cash)** — Income minus Operating Expenses minus Big Purchases, cash basis. The bottom line of your one-pager.
- **Running-month read** — the P&L read *ignoring* Big Purchases, to answer "could this month sustain itself forever?"
- **Bank-month read** — the P&L read *with* Big Purchases included, to answer "what actually happened to checking?"
- **Big Purchases spike** — a temporarily scary one-month downturn caused by a long-lived purchase. Expected; not a verdict on the business.
- **Median month** — after several months, the typical month (not the biggest or the worst). Year-one health reads better at the median than at the peaks.

## Near-but-not-quite glossary neighbors

- **LLC** — a common legal structure for small businesses (this workbook's example LLC is Asdale Dynamics, LLC). Structure questions belong with professionals.
- **EIN** — an employer ID number some businesses use; a professional can tell you if yours needs one. (Never share nor store others' business ID numbers in shared files.)
- **Depreciation** — the formal practice of spreading a big purchase's cost across years by specific rules. CPA territory; Chapter 1's "Big Purchases" bucket exists so you *don't* have to master it in year one.
- **Reconciliation software** — any tool that matches statements to books automatically. Optional; the monthly close works with paper, spreadsheet, or software alike.
